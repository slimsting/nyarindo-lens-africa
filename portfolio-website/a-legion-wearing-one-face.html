How to read this work

Everyone shown—including religious figures, public figures, fictional characters, institutions, and the author—is used here as an archetype. The images use what is known to communicate what cannot be fully known. Inclusion is symbolic; it is not a factual claim about any person, a declaration on behalf of any tradition, or an endorsement of any system.

THE SURFACE AND WHAT MOVES BENEATH IT

The Face and the Legion.

The two people in this work could be anyone. One of them could be you. The portrait is not offered as the complete self. It is the visible surface, the face through which a wider council becomes visible.

Beneath that surface are recurring roles: the Hero, Lover, Creator, Sage, Innocent, Explorer, Ruler, Caregiver, Jester, Rebel, Magician, and Everyman. They are not fixed identities. They are temporary governors of attention, need, memory, fear, and possibility.

What this image shows

This chart names twelve recurring human roles and the qualities through which they appear. The point is not to assign one permanent type, but to make the shifting cast easier to recognise.

02

TWO PEOPLE · MANY VOICES

The Meeting.

First, two people meet. Before there is a myth, there is a room: a question, an objection, a defence, a joke, a request for evidence, a plea for calm, and the feeling that everyone is somehow right and incomplete at once.

Then the ordinary meeting opens into myth. The same argument appears at a larger scale: human voices join divine figures, ancestors, animals, sun, moon, and the Child who refuses to let the meeting end.

What this image shows

This is the architecture of a human meeting. Each archetype speaks from its own responsibility and fear. The Child calls the room back with “Again!”—not because nothing changed, but because return is how understanding continues.

What this image shows

The ordinary council expands into mythic scale. Divine figures, archetypes, humans, animals, and symbols share one table. The meeting does not resolve once and for all; it remains open because life continues.

03

EVERY MAP · ONE REALITY

The Final Act.

Now the room becomes the world. Religions, philosophies, nations, institutions, economies, technologies, celebrities, fictional characters, and archetypes all arrive with maps. Everyone points. Everyone knows. No one knows the whole.

Still, Truth shows up. Wonder remains on the throne, protected by tricksters who have become experienced fools. They do not guard certainty. They guard the openness that certainty keeps trying to capture.

What this image shows

The Child sits on a throne of Wonder, surrounded by Faith, Knowledge, Wisdom, and Proof. The experienced fools and tricksters keep that openness from being captured. Every other figure represents a partial way of knowing. Truth arrives through the whole unfinished process.
